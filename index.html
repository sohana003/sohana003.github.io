About

I'm a PhD student in Computer Science at The University of Texas at San Antonio, advised by Dr. Taslima Akter in the Accessibility and Privacy Enhancing EXperience (APEX) Lab.

My research sits at the intersection of human-computer interaction, usable privacy and security, and accessibility. I study how blind and low-vision people experience the technologies they rely on every day, from messaging apps to generative AI, and where those technologies fall short in protecting their privacy and wellbeing. Through qualitative research, I examine the concerns people hold, the barriers they encounter and the strategies they develop, and turn those insights into guidance for more inclusive, privacy-preserving design.

My current work focuses on youth AI safety: how young blind and low-vision people use generative AI, the privacy and safety risks they face, and the growing role AI plays in their mental health.

Before my PhD, I spent four years as a software engineer at TigerIT Bangladesh Ltd and REVE Systems Ltd, building real-time Android messaging applications with features such as end-to-end encryption.

Research Projects

  • Safe generative AI for blind and low-vision youth Ongoing

    Young people who are blind or have low vision increasingly turn to generative AI for everyday help, including personal and emotional matters. This project studies how they use these tools, the privacy and safety risks they face, and how much they rely on AI for mental health support, toward AI that is safe for them.

  • Privacy for blind and low-vision people in instant messaging Published

    Screen readers announce messages aloud, and many privacy controls are hard to reach without sight. Through interviews with 20 blind and low-vision people, we documented their privacy concerns, the barriers they face and how they work around them, and proposed tangible privacy and security interfaces as one path forward.
